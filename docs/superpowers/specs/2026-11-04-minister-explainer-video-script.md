## 2. One platform, three levels of support (0:30–0:55)

**Narration**

> Care Bridge 360 is one application that supports people before, during and after a health concern. Prevent, Connect, Care. At the first level, Self Care: I manage my wellness with digital tools. At the second level, Virtual Care: A case manager helps me stay on track. At the third level, Direct Home Care: A qualified nurse visits at home. People move between levels as their needs change. It supports hospitals and doctors. It does not replace them.

## 3. Tier 1: the family and wellness app (0:55–1:55)

**Narration**

> Start with Self Care. Each day, the parent does a simple check-in. How they slept, how they feel, in a few taps. Medicines are scheduled, doses are marked off, and the app shows when stock is running low, so a refill is never missed. Blood pressure, sugar, heart rate and oxygen build into a clear picture. A connected smartwatch feeds heart rate and oxygen readings in automatically, so the picture stays current without anyone typing. The care team is one tap away. Health records live in one place, kept securely in an Indian data centre, with access controls on every record. And with the parent's permission, the son, thousands of kilometres away, opens the app and sees today: she checked in, she took her morning tablets, her readings are steady. Not a flood of data. Just reassurance.

Vitals and notes go straight into the member's record, so the family, with consent, sees how the visit went. Home visits begin only where trained staff and supervision are ready.

## 6. When something goes wrong (3:15–3:45)

**Narration**

> Now, a hard moment. She feels unwell. In a life-threatening emergency, the first call is always emergency services. We know connectivity in some places is patchy, so Care Bridge 360 does not promise an emergency response. What it can do is put help within reach: the nearest ambulance and GP for her area, one tap away, coming with our January launch. And when she does press SOS, an alert with her location goes to her case manager's inbox, so her care team knows and can follow up. Alerts to the family's phone are the next thing we are building.

## Style notes

- Voice: calm, clear, unhurried, mid-pace. Any accent (Santhosh, 2026-11-14). Higgsfield preset voice.
- Music: soft instrumental, ducked under voice, rising slightly in section 7.
- Captions: burned in, white on soft dark band, max two lines.
- AI character consistency: generate one reference each for the mother, son, nurse and coordinator first, and reuse across clips.
- Kerala cues kept respectful: home interiors, cotton saree and mundu, palms, rain-washed light. No clichés (no elephants, no houseboats).
